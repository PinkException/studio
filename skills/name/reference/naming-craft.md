# Naming craft — what sticks, what kills, how to score

A condensed field guide for **final mode**. Adapted, with thanks, from the MIT-licensed
[glacierphonk/naming](https://github.com/glacierphonk/naming) skill, trimmed to what a
small studio naming consumer apps actually needs.

## What makes a name stick

1. **A metaphor you can draw.** The best names borrow a concrete image from another
   domain: Sentry (a guard → error monitoring), Docker (dock worker → containers), Vault.
   A description just labels the category: SiteWatch, DataSync. Test: *can you draw a
   picture of the name?*
2. **Real words win recall.** Known words repurposed beat coined words roughly two to
   one in recall tests. Invented words must be short and rooted in morphemes people
   recognise (Figma ← figure, Grafana ← graph). "Nexagen" is noise.
3. **Both halves of a compound must earn their place.** Cover one half: does the other
   still say something specific? Datadog, Mailchimp yes; CloudSync, SmartHub no.
4. **Length.** One or two syllables ideal, three is the practical ceiling for one word.
   Four to six letters ideal; seven max for an invented word.
5. **The phone test.** Say it once to someone. Can they repeat it, spell it closely
   enough to search for it, and remember it tomorrow? Clever misspellings fail this.
6. **A 15-second origin story.** If you can't explain why it's called that, it isn't
   ready. The story is also free marketing copy.
7. **Distinctive over descriptive.** The name creates recall; the tagline creates
   clarity. Slack, Notion, Discord say nothing about their category and stick anyway.

### Extra gates for a small consumer-app studio

- **Market languages.** Say it aloud in every language on the roadmap. Check that it
  isn't a rude, medical, or silly word in any of them (quick WebSearch:
  `"<name>" meaning french`, `"<name>" signification`, `"<name>" bedeutung`). Avoid
  diacritics and sounds that one of the languages can't produce.
- **Family fit.** If the studio has a recognisable style (warm, a little playful, short),
  the product name should feel like a sibling, not a stranger — without needing the
  studio's name bolted on.
- **Spoken by a tired person.** Consumer apps are recommended across a kitchen table,
  not in a boardroom. "You should try ___" must sound natural.
- **Searchable.** A common dictionary word with a famous namesake (a city, a celebrity,
  a Wikipedia topic) will bury the app in search. Score it down; don't auto-kill.

## What kills a name

**AI slop patterns** — if a name matches three or more, drop it:

| Pattern | Examples | Why it's dead |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Suffix addiction | Glidely, Parsify, Syncora, Buildly | Thousands already did it |
| Meaningless portmanteau | Brightrix, Nexagen, Vaultora | No story, no image |
| Thesaurus words | Apex, Zenith, Summit, Catalyst | Owned by a million companies |
| Category + modifier | CloudGuard, DataSync, TaskTrack | Forgettable description |
| Corporate filler | X Solutions, X Labs, X Hub, X Platform | Communicates nothing |
| Vowel-dropping | Flickr, Tumblr-style | Signals "the real spelling was taken" |
| Fake Latin/Greek | Cognivex, Synaptiq, Luminex | Impressive for two seconds |
| Aspirational adjectives | BrightPath, TrueNorth, ClearView | Could be any product |

**Filler words.** First position: Smart, Auto, Cloud, AI, Data, Next, Digital, True,
Pure, Easy, Pro, Ultra, Super, Meta, Core. Second position: Hub, Lab, Base, Flow, Sync,
Link, Stack, Forge, Verse, Path, Nest, Box. If removing a word doesn't change the
meaning, the word is filler.

**Red-flag checklist** — two or more "yes" answers disqualify a candidate:

- Does someone hear it and say "oh, like [ten other products]"?
- Can you *not* tell a 15-second story about it?
- Does it need spelling out letter by letter?
- Does it only work in writing, not aloud?
- Is it just a cool word with no link to the product?
- Would it "work" just as well on a different product in a different category?
- Strip the styling (caps, symbols, letter swaps): is it now generic?
- Is its main virtue that the domain was free?
- Does a common meaning of the word contradict what the product does?
- Does it mean something unfortunate in a market language?

**The "so what?" test.** Explain the origin story. "That's clever" → ship it. "Oh, I
see" → acceptable. "So what?" / "I don't get it" / "Isn't that already a thing?" → dead.

## How to score finalists

Only names that cleared the red-flag checklist get scored. Rate 1–5 each, then weight.

| Criterion | Weight | Ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Metaphor strength | ×3 | Does it plant a concrete image tied to the product? |
| Memorability | ×3 | Remembered tomorrow after one hearing? |
| Story | ×2 | Does the origin story land ("that's clever")? |
| Distinctiveness | ×2 | Stands apart from competitors and neighbours? |
| Phone test | ×2 | Spellable after hearing, sayable after reading? |
| Searchability | ×2 | Can someone find it, or is the word owned by something famous? |
| Market languages | ×2 | Sayable and harmless in every roadmap language? |
| Length | ×1 | 1–2 syllables, under 7 letters? |
| Sound fit | ×1 | Do the sounds match the product's character? |
| Availability | ×1 | Domains, stores, handles — see checks.md |
| Family fit | ×1 | Feels like a sibling of the studio's other names? |
| Longevity | ×1 | Still fine in ten years? No era-marker like "AI"? |

Maximum 105. Above 75 is strong; above 85 is exceptional. A high total with one fatal
weakness (phone test 2, a live competitor) still loses — the table makes trade-offs
visible, it doesn't replace judgment.

**Sentence tests** for every finalist, aloud:

- "Have you tried ___?"  ·  "I've used ___ for a month."
- "___ is a <category> that <does what>."
- "the ___ app" · "open ___" · "___ reminded me"
- "___ — <tagline>" · "Get ___ on Google Play"
- "You should try this thing called ___." ← the one that matters most

**Present side by side.** Name, origin story, territory, availability in one line,
trade-offs, recommendation. Three to five names. The user decides.
